* Problem 86 :dynamic_programming:graph:dag:longest_path:
** Task
#+begin_quote
Longest simple path in a directed acyclic graph: A directed graph $\Gamma$ is a graph, where every edge has a direction. Mathematically, that means that $\Gamma = (V, E)$, with a finite set $V$ of vertices and a finite set $E \subseteq V \times V$ of directed edges. Note that edges of directed graphs are ordered pairs, so $(i, j) \neq (j, i)$ if $i \neq j$. A path of length $k$ in a directed graph is a sequence of vertices $v_0 v_1 v_2 \ldots v_k$, where $(v_{i-1}, v_i) \in E$ for all $i = 1, \ldots, k$. A path is called a cycle, if $v_0 = v_k$. A directed graph without cycles is called a directed acyclic graph, short DAG.

You are given a directed acyclic graph $\Gamma = (V, E)$ with real-valued edge weights, i.e., every edge $e \in E$ has a weight $w_e \in \mathbb{R}$, and two distinguished vertices $s$ and $t$. The weight of a path is the sum of the weights of the edges in the path. Describe a dynamic-programmingr approach for finding a longest weighted simple path from $s$ to $t$. What is the running time of your algorithm?
#+end_quote

* Problem 88 :dynamic_programming:optimization:inventory:
** Task
#+begin_quote
he Rinky Dink Company makes machines that resurface ice rinks. The demand for such products varies from month to month, and so the company needs to develop a strategy to plan its manufacturing given the fluctuating, but predictable, demand. The company wishes to design a plan for the next $n$ months. For each month $i$, the company knows the demand $d_i$, that is, the number of machines that it will sell. Let $D = \sum_{i=1}^{n} d_i$ be the total demand over the next $n$ months. The company keeps a full-time staff who provide labor to manufacture up to $m$ machines per month. If the company needs to make more than $m$ machines in a given month, it can hire additional, part-time labor, at a cost that works out to $c$ dollars per machine. Furthermore, if the company is holding any unsold machines at the end of a month, it must pay inventory costs. The company can hold up to $D$ machines, with the cost for holding $j$ machines given as a function $h(j)$ for $j = 1, 2, \ldots, D$ that monotonically increases with $j$. Give an algorithm that calculates a plan for the company that minimizes its costs while fulfilling all the demand. The running time should be polynomial in $n$ and $D$.
#+end_quote

More formally, given an array $L[1 : m]$ containing the break points for a string of $n$ characters, compute the lowest cost for a sequence of breaks, along with a sequence of breaks that achieves this cost.
